## Platform

## Stack

Jekyll, hosted on GitHub Pages (user's choice: "Jekyll academic template"). Custom theme rather than a stock academic theme. Only GitHub Pages-supported plugins (jekyll-seo-tag, jekyll-sitemap). Domain: wyattkriebel.com, registered at GoDaddy.

## Users

Two audiences, weighted equally:
- Academic: faculty and researchers in protein engineering and gene therapy who look Wyatt up after seeing his name on a paper, poster or email.
- Industry: biotech recruiters and hiring managers evaluating him for research or bioprocess roles.

Both arrive from a link (CV, email signature, paper, search for his name) and want to answer, within a minute: who is this, what has he done, what can he do, and how do I reach him.

## Product Purpose

The personal professional site of Wyatt G. Kriebel. It makes his research record and skills easy to verify and gives both audiences a credible place to land. Success: a reader leaves knowing his research arc (retinal gene therapy, then computational enzyme design, then cGMP manufacturing), has opened the publications, and knows how to contact him.

## Positioning

Few early-career candidates combine all three: wet-lab gene therapy research (AAV vector design and purification, behavioral phenotyping), computational work (MD simulation, docking, scRNA-seq pipelines, de novo enzyme simulation), and cGMP manufacturing experience including FDA-facing process qualification. The site should make that bench-to-computation-to-manufacturing span legible.

## Operating Context

Visitors are often on a laptop between other application materials, or on a phone after a conference or email. Publications must link to DOIs. Content is maintained by editing Markdown or YAML files in the repo.

## Product Principles

1. Verifiable over impressive: every claim traces to a paper, DOI or role.
2. Scientific credibility first: no marketing tone, no startup gloss.
3. Skimmable in a minute, complete in five.
4. Distinct, not a stock academic theme, while staying conventional enough that faculty find what they expect where they expect it.
